# The research behind Shan'do and Herbalore

Shan'do is a night elf spirit guide and Herbalore gives every herb a voice. Both were built for immersion,
meditation, reflection, repair and growth while playing a night elf. This page records the evidence behind each
choice: what the guide does, why, and how sure we can be.

## How to read this

- **Evidence levels** are written as words. *Confirmed*: replicated or meta-analytic and holding up. *Supported*:
  good evidence, with caveats. *Hypothesis*: plausible, untested here. *Refuted*: failed when tested properly.
- **What was read.** Every DOI was checked against Crossref (or Europe PMC / OpenAlex) in November 2026. No paper was
  read in full. Each reference says what was read: the abstract, the metadata only, or a secondary summary.
- **Effect sizes** are given as reported (g, d, r, OR) with intervals where the source had them.

## The big caution

Effects in this field shrink when they are tested properly. Correcting the positive-psychology meta-analyses for
small-study bias leaves about r = .10 on wellbeing and little on depression (White et al., 2019). Many Labs 2 found
a median replication effect of d = 0.15, against 0.60 in the original studies (Klein et al., 2018).

So the guide promises little and invites gently. There are **no trials of voiced, in-game wellbeing prompts**:
every rule about how a voice should prompt is carried over from other settings, and is a hypothesis here. She is
company, not therapy.

## 2. The sacred, belonging and hierarchy

**Nature as the sacred, and where corruption lives.** The neat mapping of disgust onto "divinity" (Rozin et al., 1999)
does not hold in its strong form; violations draw mixed emotions (Kollareth & Russell, 2017), and incidental disgust
doesn't harden moral judgement after bias correction (Landy & Goodwin, 2015). Self-disgust, though, predicted later
depression in a three-wave study (Powell et al., 2013; review: Clarke et al., 2019). So **corruption belongs to
places, never to you.** Felwood is *wounded, not wicked*; the sickness is in the land, not in you. The sacred comes
through nature, awe and elevation, never through purity talk aimed at the self. The Highborne ruins ask for pity for
their hunger, not disgust.

**Belonging and chosen family.** The need to belong (Baumeister & Leary, 1995) is supported; relationships and
survival (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010) confirmed. Kin lines use *Ishnu-dal-dieb* (good fortune to your family) and the
lore of chosen family: Shandris, raised by Tyrande. Coming home to Darnassus has its own lines.

**Hierarchy without shame.** Shame tracks depression more than guilt does: r = .43 against .28, across 108 studies
(Kim et al., 2011). Guilt is about the act and leads to repair (Tangney et al., 2007). Defeat and entrapment relate
to depression (Gilbert & Allan, 1998); respect freely given predicts wellbeing (Anderson et al., 2015). So she speaks
as *shan'do* to *thero'shan*, teacher to student: earned warmth, never dominance. Setbacks are repair (*Andu-falah-dor*,
let balance be restored), never being lesser. After three deaths in fifteen minutes she offers one way out ("the
grove will wait") and then stays quiet about dying. At trainers, learning is an honour, not a lessening.

**Moral Foundations** are used as descriptions only: the five-factor questionnaire fails measurement invariance across
27 countries (Iurino & Saucier, 2020), and the MFQ-2 is newer (Atari et al., 2023).

## 3. Loss, and healing

Some players carry loss, or a hard start in life. The guide may speak to that, but only with hope, gently, about
healing, grounded in the evidence, and in night elf terms.

| What a line offers | Evidence | Level |
|---|---|---|
| You're not alone in it | Common humanity is part of self-compassion; compassion-based interventions d 0.64 (Kirby et al., 2017) | supported |
| Safety can be learned later, from whoever offers it (Shandris, raised by Tyrande) | Earned security (Roisman et al., 2002); attachment only moderately stable from infancy to adulthood (Pinquart et al., 2013) | supported |
| Speak to an old ache the way she speaks to you | Compassion-focused therapy builds a compassionate inner voice (Gilbert, 2014) | supported (mechanism); hypothesis in a game |
| Grief comes and goes; setting it down is allowed | The dual process model: moving between loss and everyday life (Stroebe & Schut, 1999) | supported (model) |
| Healing is made of ordinary things | Resilience as "ordinary magic" (Masten, 2001) | supported |
| The wisps: love lives on in what it grew | Continuing bonds help some people and not others (Hewson et al., 2023; Stroebe & Schut, 2005), so this is offered as a way of seeing, never as advice to hold on | mixed |

**When:** only inside a ritual you chose (a quarter of barrow-den dreams, a third of moonlit vigils), at most once
a day, and one in the Children's Week pool. Never unprompted. Children's Week otherwise gets gentle lines about play,
wonder and kindness given. Nothing promises growth from loss (see section 4).

## 4. *The Happiness Hypothesis* (Haidt, 2006): what was followed, what was dropped

The book's structure came from a library catalogue record, Wikipedia and the author's page; the book text itself was
not read for this record.

| Idea | Evidence today | Level | Used? |
|---|---|---|---|
| The elephant and the rider | A metaphor; dual-process theory defended (Evans & Stanovich, 2013) and attacked (Melnikoff & Bargh, 2018) | hypothesis | Herbalore's quiet lines speak to habit and rhythm, not willpower |
| Changing your mind | CBT g 0.79 against control (Cuijpers et al., 2023); MBCT relapse HR 0.69 (Kuyken et al., 2016) | supported | Only gentle, non-clinical forms: pauses and noticing |
| Reciprocity | Social bonds and health (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010) | supported | Kindness and kin lines |
| H = S + C + V, and the "40%" pie | No empirical basis (Brown & Rohrer, 2020); withdrawn as a diagram by its authors (Sheldon & Lyubomirsky, 2021); set points do move (Lucas, 2007; Diener et al., 2006) | refuted (40%) | **Dropped.** No promise of a share of happiness in your hands, no "you'll bounce back" |
| Control matters | Uncontrollable noise costs more (Glass et al., 1971); the commuting paradox (Stutzer & Frey, 2008) | supported | `/shando quieter` and `/shando nearer`; everything optional |
| The uses of adversity | Self-reported growth isn't measured change (Frazier et al., 2009); no genuine growth on average (Mangelsdorf et al., 2019); the "growth illusion" (Infurna & Jayawickreme, 2019) | refuted as a promise | **Dropped.** Hard moments get company, not a lesson |
| The felicity of virtue | Seligman et al. (2005) gains matched by placebo in replication (Mongrain & Anselmo-Matthews, 2012); g 0.42 (Schutte & Malouff, 2019) | supported, small | Level-ups name a strength shown (patience, care, curiosity) |
| Divinity with or without God | Elevation, short term (Pohling & Diessner, 2016); awe (Keltner & Haidt, 2003) | supported (short term) | Places, kindness, the moon |
| Happiness comes from between | Vital engagement (Nakamura & Csikszentmihalyi, 2003); Haidt's own synthesis | hypothesis | Love (kin), work (professions), something larger (Elune, the forest) |

## 5. How she speaks

1. **Invite, never instruct:** "if you like", "perhaps" (Ryan & Deci, 2000; Ntoumanis et al., 2021; on reactance,
   Steindl et al., 2015). A few short favourites, such as "Breathe. The forest isn't going anywhere.", were kept as
   they were.
2. **No affirmations about the self.** Positive self-statements made people with low self-esteem feel worse (Wood et
   al., 2009). Lines speak about the moment, the world and the deed.
3. **Accept feelings, don't fix them** (Ford et al., 2018); valuing happiness can backfire (Mauss et al., 2011).
4. **Concrete, not abstract,** reflection (Watkins, 2008; Nolen-Hoeksema et al., 2008).
5. **Short and rare.** At least 45 s between any two lines, shared between the addons. Each line rests 30 minutes
   after it's said, then 60, then 120 (Herbalore: 20, 40, 80), so nothing repeats through an evening. The right rate
   is unknown: a hypothesis.
6. **Never in combat or dungeons.** One line per death; one repair line after repeated deaths, then quiet.
7. **No claims of therapy.** Meditation can have adverse effects (Farias et al., 2020), so nothing long or guided
   beyond a 2½-minute breath, and one command turns her off.
8. **Stay in Azeroth.** No line mentions the real world, and no line borrows another author's words.

**Left out because it failed or is weak:** ego depletion (Hagger et al., 2016; Vohs et al., 2021), refuted; power
posing on hormones and behaviour (Ranehill et al., 2015), refuted; pen-in-mouth facial feedback (Wagenmakers et al.,
2016), refuted, with deliberate smiling small (Coles et al., 2022); binaural beats (Garcia-Argibay et al., 2019;
Ingendoh et al., 2023; Platt & Hammond, 2024), weak, with the mechanism mostly unsupported.

## 6. Sound

**Everything except the voice is synthesised from scratch** with numpy: no samples and no model. A two-bell chime, a
single bell, a rising swell of low fifths for sacred places, the breathing wind, a wordless choir for the wisp, and
60-second looping beds (inn by day and night, Shadowmeld by day and night, the Dream, the moonlit vigil). Beds loop
seamlessly because noise is shaped on the frequency grid of the whole clip and events wrap round the end. Levels sit
well below the voice and play on the player's own Ambience and SFX sliders (water about level with the background,
never above it: Galbrun & Ali, 2013).

**The Shadowmeld breath is also visual:** a violet edge on the screen brightens on each in-breath and fades on the
out-breath, in step with the wind. Whether a visual pacer helps more than sound alone is a hypothesis.

**The voice** is AI-generated with VoxCPM2 (Apache-2.0): designed once from a written description and used as the
reference for every line, so all lines share one voice, with a soft reverb.

**Generation models considered** for richer beds (checked against model cards, repos and papers, November 2026):

## 7. Night elf culture, and its sources

Lore came from search results quoting warcraft.wiki.gg, Wowhead, Blizzard Watch's "Know Your Lore" and the Vanilla
WoW archive. Level: supported unless noted.

- **Darnassian:** *Ishnu-alah* (good fortune to you), *Ishnu-dal-dieb* (good fortune to your family), *Elune-adore*
  (Elune be with you), *Andu-falah-dor* (let balance be restored), *Ande'thoras-ethil* (may your troubles be
  diminished), *shan'do* / *thero'shan* (honoured teacher / student), *kaldorei* (children of the stars).
  *Shorel'aran* is Thalassian, not Darnassian, so it isn't used. "Mother Moon" for Elune had no authoritative source,
  so it isn't used either.
- **Druids** slept for centuries in barrow dens, their minds in the Emerald Dream; Cenarius is their patron and
  Malfurion his student. Moonglade is their neutral sanctuary.
- **The Sentinels** are led by Shandris Feathermoon, raised by Tyrande.
- **Wisps** are the spirits of night elf dead who have become one with the forest; on Mount Hyjal the Ancestral
  Spirits gave themselves to destroy Archimonde.
- **The Highborne's** hunger for the Well, the Sundering and the exile are told with pity, not disgust.
- **Felwood** is a corrupted forest, with Emerald Sanctuary druids healing it and the Shadow Council at Jaedenar.
- **Festivals:** the Lunar Festival honours the Elders, with the druids of Moonglade at its heart; the Harvest
  Festival remembers the fallen; Children's Week gives back to the young.
- **WoW: Forever's herbalism** (from several player guides): new herbs Marefoil, Demonsage and Death Lotus (Mount
  Hyjal), scarce finds (Frilled Lichen, Rosecap, Stranglevine) and four tiers of seeds grown in a greenhouse. The new
  herbs had no published lore, so their lines describe rather than invent.
- **Plant facts** in Herbalore are general botany in plain words: moss drinking through its leaves, lichen as two
  lives in one, grass growing from its base, lotus leaves shedding water, seeds waiting for a good year, fungi trading
  with roots. The popular idea that trees "talk" through fungal networks is disputed, so it isn't used.

## 8. Testing

**In the game** (WoW: Forever, 6 November 2026), the saved variables record each line with a timestamp. Confirmed
working: place lines (subzone names match), stillness and breath, kindness on others, level-ups, zone lines,
Shadowmeld meditation (with the music restored afterwards), herb lines with their rests and least-heard-first choice,
skinning thanks, quiet lines and the herb notebook. Two bugs were found this way and fixed: the login greeting lost
its turn to a zone line fired at login, and the session look-back was never saved because each `/reload` restarted
its clock.

**In a mock of the WoW API**, a simulated session drives every feature: places, stillness, forms, skills, deaths,
kindness, groups, trainers, inns, dusk, late nights, all four rituals, the look-back across a reload, and festivals.
It caught five more bugs before they reached the game.

**Not yet seen in the game:** forms, profession milestones, the quest chime, flights, inns, deaths, groups, trainers,
dawn and dusk, the Dream, the vigil, the wisp, festivals and the Shadowmeld breathing light.
